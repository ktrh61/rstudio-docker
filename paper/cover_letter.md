Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Driver-conditioned transcriptomic differences across radiation-attributability bands in papillary thyroid carcinoma", for publication in the *British Journal of Cancer* as an original research article.

Papillary thyroid carcinoma after the Chernobyl accident remains a major human model of radiation carcinogenesis, yet whether exposure is associated with tumor expression within a driver stratum has remained unresolved. Because driver prevalence varies with radiation dose, pooled exposed-versus-unexposed comparisons can reproduce differences between fusion- and point-mutation-driven tumors rather than an exposure-associated difference within a comparable molecular background. Using open-access RNA-seq from the 440-case REBC-THYR cohort, we compared cases in the high NIH IREP Assigned Share band (High-AS) with dose-zero cases separately within RET fusion-positive and BRAF V600E-positive strata; the source cohort's continuous dose–expression analyses had not identified a corresponding signal. High-AS RET fusion-positive tumors differed broadly in expression (1,765 genes at Storey q<0.10; Higher Criticism p=0.0112), whereas the other three contrasts lacked concordant gene- and contrast-level evidence. Together, the gene- and contrast-level results provide positive evidence that expression differs by attributability band within RET fusion-positive PTCs; they do not establish a radiation-specific signature. An REO panel derived from the extreme RET bands was applied without refitting to intermediate-band tumors, yielding a descriptive ordered profile that requires independent validation. Gene-level p-values were obtained by exhaustive label enumeration, and the prespecified contrast- and set-level tests were calibrated by label permutation. The complete pipeline reproduced identical primary artifacts on two machines, and the analysis code, versioned inputs, and container recipe accompany the paper.

The manuscript builds on work published in the *British Journal of Cancer*. Whether post-Chernobyl thyroid cancers bear a radiation-specific expression signature was examined genome-wide in this journal by Detours et al. (2005, 2007), and two of the studies against which we cross-reference our results — the normal-tissue signature of Dom et al. (2012) and the iodine-131 dose-dependent expression changes of Abend et al. (2013) — were also published here. What has changed since is the demonstration, in the REBC-THYR cohort, that driver prevalence itself varies with radiation dose (Morton et al. 2021). Whether the earlier expression signals persist within driver strata has therefore been unclear, and this manuscript asks that question in the same cohort.

Beyond thyroid cancer, this driver-conditioned extreme-band framework may be applicable where a case-level exposure metric is available and oncogenic drivers structure molecular profiles. We therefore believe the results and framework will be of interest to the journal's readership in cancer epidemiology, cancer genomics, biostatistics, and thyroid oncology.

It reports original research that has not been published previously and has not been posted as a preprint, and it is not under consideration for publication elsewhere.
