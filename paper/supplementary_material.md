## Supplementary Methods

### Data sources and expression matrix

The National Cancer Institute (NCI) Genomic Data Commons (GDC) API (Heath et al. 2021) was queried for open-access files in project REBC-THYR under GDC Data Release 45.0 <!-- N-92 -->, using data category Transcriptome Profiling, experimental strategy RNA-Seq, data type Gene Expression Quantification, and workflow type STAR - Counts. The query returned 906 released files <!-- N-89 -->. The accompanying manifest contained the file UUIDs, names, sizes, and GDC-provided MD5 checksums (manifest MD5, 7defb0c5574453474c67dfac8367a589) <!-- N-89 -->; every downloaded file was verified against its recorded checksum.

Clinical data came from Data S1 of Morton et al. (2021), which we read with all columns retained and without editing any values. Missing markers were converted to NA, and a column was treated as numeric only when every non-missing value parsed as numeric.

Files were mapped to cases and biospecimens through the GDC API using GenomicDataCommons (Morgan and Davis 2025), and one count assay per sample was assembled in a SummarizedExperiment container (Morgan et al. 2025). Removing all-zero genes yielded 58,448 genes and 906 samples <!-- N-14 -->. Exon-union gene lengths were derived from the GDC GENCODE v36 reference annotation (Mudge et al. 2025) using rtracklayer (Lawrence et al. 2009) <!-- N-66 -->.

For each sample, library strandedness was determined from the totals of the two STAR stranded columns. A library was classified as stranded when the smaller total was no more than half the larger. The larger column (for stranded libraries) or the unstranded column (otherwise) was taken as the sample's counts <!-- N-67 -->. All 906 libraries were classified as reverse stranded; their smaller-to-larger ratios ranged from 0.056 to 0.110, well below the 0.5 threshold <!-- N-77 -->. GDC TPM and FPKM columns were not used. For the REO analysis, TPM was recalculated from the selected count assay and exon-union gene lengths.

### Exposure metric: Assigned Share

NIH IREP version 5.7.3 (Kocher et al. 2008) was used to calculate Assigned Share associated with the expected value of the excess relative risk. The fixed inputs were electrons E>15 keV, acute exposure rate, organ dose entered as a constant in cSv=mGy/10, recorded sex, exposure year 1986, birth year calculated as 1986 minus age at exposure, and surgery year calculated as birth year plus age at surgery. All other settings remained at the program defaults: user-defined uncertainty distribution Lognormal(1,1), 10,000 iterations, and random-number seed 99 <!-- N-68 -->. The dose and age inputs in the versioned AS file were verified case by case against the clinical table.

IREP guidance identifies iodine-131 as an example of the electrons E>15 keV route (National Cancer Institute n.d.). This route has a radiation effectiveness factor of 1.0 relative to reference higher-energy photons (Land et al. 2003) <!-- N-83 -->, with independent microdosimetric support for similar biological effectiveness of internal iodine-131 and external gamma rays per absorbed dose (Sato et al. 2014). This addresses radiation quality, not the transfer of a thyroid risk model fitted to external photon exposures. The IREP thyroid model is based on atomic-bomb survivors and populations irradiated as children for medical indications (Ron et al. 1995; Kocher et al. 2008).

We retained the acute setting to preserve computational continuity with earlier IREP applications to post-Chornobyl papillary carcinoma from the same research program (Zurnadzhy et al. 2022; Bogdanova et al. 2022). In IREP, exposure rate affects the calculation through the dose and dose-rate effectiveness factor. Under the acute setting the factor approaches 1 at and above an uncertain reference dose distributed log-uniformly from 0.03 to 0.2 Gy <!-- N-83 -->; the minimum dose in the High-AS band was 188 mGy <!-- N-84 -->. A chronic calculation lowers the numeric AS scale. Because the High-AS minimum dose lies near the upper end of this range, a chronic calculation would compress the AS scale across the High-AS band by essentially the same factor for all cases and could not materially reorder cases. The acute setting thus defined the numerical AS scale used for band construction. It was not intended to reconstruct the temporal pattern of internal iodine-131 dose delivery.

Dose was entered as a point value, and dosimetric or risk-model uncertainty was not propagated into band membership. Dose-zero cases formed the separate reference group. Exposed cases were classified as Low (0<AS<33.3), Mid (33.3≤AS<66.6), and High (AS≥66.6); no case lay on a boundary <!-- N-69 -->. The cut-points were operational boundaries on the specified acute-calculation scale and were not interpreted as biological or etiological probability thresholds. The stratum-by-band pools from which the analyzed groups were drawn are given in Table 2 <!-- N-95 -->. We calculated AS for every exposed case in the two analysis strata (57 RET fusion-positive and 142 BRAF V600E-positive cases) <!-- N-93 -->. Cases outside these strata were not included in any analysis.

### Quality control and analysis cohorts

Within each observed group-by-tissue submatrix, genes were filtered with edgeR's filterByExpr (Chen et al. 2016; Chen et al. 2025) before principal-component-scores-based outlier detection (PC-OD). We retained the pre-filter library sizes as the counts-per-million denominators and used unnormalized log counts per million with prior count 2 so that composition anomalies were not first attenuated by between-sample normalization. At each iteration, the most extreme sample on the first principal component was removed using the two-sided PC-OD rule at α=0.05, and the procedure continued until no further sample was rejected (Nakayama et al. 2024) <!-- N-78 -->. PC-OD was a quality-control (QC) and cohort-definition step, and its finalized flags were held fixed in downstream inference. It flagged no tumor or normal sample in the RET fusion-positive stratum. Its only flag among the four main target groups was one High-AS BRAF V600E-positive tumor <!-- N-87 -->.

Purity estimation followed PC-OD so that an anomalous sample could not affect the purity fit. We estimated relative tumor purity jointly for dose-zero and High-AS cases within each driver cohort using an in-house implementation of the initial matched-pair proportion score from contamDE-lm rather than its iterative pseudo-likelihood differential-expression model (Shen et al. 2016; Ji et al. 2020). Paired counts of protein-coding genes retained by filterByExpr were MUREN-normalized (Feng and Li 2021). A paired limma-voom model (Law et al. 2014; Ritchie et al. 2015) with robust empirical Bayes (Phipson et al. 2016) selected the up- and down-regulated working genes used to construct the score, which was rescaled so that the cohort maximum was 1. Working genes were those with Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted p<0.1 and |log2 fold change|>log2(1.5); when more than 1,000 genes met the adjusted-p criterion, the 1,000 with the smallest adjusted p-values were retained. The paired log2 tumor-to-normal ratios for the proportion score were computed with a pseudo-count of 1, and the empirical Bayes fit used the mean–variance trend in addition to robust estimation. The limma-voom fit served only this purity-estimation step and was not used for the reported gene-level inference. Joint estimation placed both groups on a common relative scale; this assumes a common, driver-dominated tumor reference within each driver cohort. We treated purity as a relative score within the jointly estimated cases, not as an absolute cellular fraction or a device for separating purity from all AS-aligned expression structure.

Case pairing used the GDC merged-aliquot expression assays. Each included case was required to have one unique merged tumor and one unique merged normal sample. The main cohort additionally required RET fusion-positive classification or BRAF V600E-positive classification without another candidate driver mutation in the source cohort's whole-genome sequencing (WGS) or RNA calls (175 of 190 BRAF V600E cases <!-- N-94 -->), membership in the dose-zero or High-AS group, both tissues passing the outlier screen, and relative purity of at least 0.6.

The REO construction set comprised the RET fusion-positive subset of the main cohort. The REO application set comprised RET fusion-positive PTCs from the Low-AS and Mid-AS bands, whether or not a matched normal was available. We did not apply the outlier and purity exclusions of the construction cohort to this set, so that no case was removed from the set to which the panel was applied. The corresponding metrics were reported as descriptive diagnostics and were not used to exclude cases.

### Analysis contrasts

The four High-AS versus dose-zero contrasts were RET-tumor (`R_Tumor` in the analysis files), RET-normal (`R_Normal`), BRAF-tumor (`B_Tumor`), and BRAF-normal (`B_Normal`). These labels refer to contrasts, not sample groups. In each contrast, group X was dose-zero and group Y was High-AS. The Brunner–Munzel relative effect (probabilistic index; Brunner and Munzel 2000) is denoted θ:

θ = Pr(X<Y) + 0.5 Pr(X=Y).

Values of θ>0.5 indicate that expression tends to be higher in High-AS cases. The signed effect shown in Figure 2 was 2θ−1. Higher Criticism (Donoho and Jin 2004) was the single primary contrast-level test for RET-tumor. The corresponding tests in the other three contrasts addressed distinct secondary questions. We reported all four omnibus p-values separately and did not combine them into a study-level decision. Accordingly, no across-contrast multiplicity adjustment was applied.

### Covariate disclosure and estimand

For exposed cases, age at exposure entered the IREP model as a radiation-risk modifier. Dose-zero cases had no corresponding age at exposure, so this variable was not compared between groups. The observable age structure in each driver stratum was characterized using the Hodges–Lehmann median difference (Hodges and Lehmann 1963) in age at surgery and the Brunner–Munzel relative effect defined above. These complementary rank-based summaries described group separation on the original scale (years) and on a probability scale without imposing a normal model. For both estimates, 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (Efron and Tibshirani 1993) were obtained from 9,999 resamples drawn separately within each group (seed 19450809) <!-- N-63 -->. No p-value was calculated.

We reported sex by group because expression of sex-chromosome genes can differ with group composition. Using GENCODE v36 chromosome assignments, we counted X- and Y-linked genes among all genes tested in each contrast and among the genes meeting the Storey q<0.10 rule, with the latter also separated by effect direction; no chromosome-enrichment test was performed. In the finalized IREP calculations, the AS values were numerically insensitive to the recorded sex input; sex was therefore not regarded as absorbed into the exposure metric. RET fusion-partner counts were reported by band; the BRAF stratum consisted of BRAF V600E-positive cases by construction. We fitted no common covariate-adjusted model: conditioning on the age inputs used to construct AS would change the marginal AS-band estimand, relative purity was expression-derived and used for eligibility, and fusion partner may be part of the biological pathway under study.

The finalized analysis objects contained no sequencing-batch, processing-batch, center, or collection-period field suitable for a group-balance calculation. This unavailable information is treated as an unassessed source of technical confounding. Available relative-purity, age, sex, and fusion-partner variables were reported descriptively without an additional hypothesis test.

### Normalization

For each contrast, counts were restricted to protein-coding genes and edgeR's filterByExpr was applied again, using the two contrast groups, before normalization with an in-house three-iteration implementation of DEGES-MUREN (Kadota et al. 2012; Sun et al. 2013; Feng and Li 2021). The iteration scheme and the floorPDEG guard follow the iDEGES procedure of the TCC package (Sun et al. 2013). The package itself was not used, and the differential-expression screen inside each iteration was replaced as described below. The reimplementation initializes the worker random-number streams for least-trimmed-squares subsampling with a fixed implementation-level seed (12345 <!-- N-97 -->), separate from the seeds used for permutation and bootstrap procedures. This initialization applies to all MUREN calls, including those used for relative-purity estimation. In this two-group use, filterByExpr uses the group sizes to set the required number of expressed samples but does not use the identities of the samples meeting that expression rule. For fixed group sizes, the retained set is therefore invariant to relabeling. The MUREN step used its single-parameter pairwise least-trimmed-squares form, implemented with `MASS::ltsreg` (Venables and Ripley 2002), the engine used by the original MUREN implementation. At each iteration, a Brunner–Munzel permutation screen enumerated all label allocations and produced Storey q-values (Storey 2002) with λ=0.5. This screen replaced the model-based differential-expression screen in the original DEGES scheme. The larger of the q<0.10 set and the top 5% of genes by raw p-value was removed from scaling-factor estimation, implementing the floorPDEG guard <!-- N-71 -->. Tested-gene counts, screen π0 estimates, Jaccard convergence, and scaling-factor ranges are given in Table S1 <!-- N-15 -->.

Because the DEGES exclusion screen used the observed group labels and normalization was not recomputed for each label allocation, the gene-level exhaustive enumeration treated the finalized normalized matrix as fixed.

### Gene-level differential expression

For each gene, the studentized Brunner–Munzel statistic (two-sided; Neubert and Brunner 2007) was evaluated for every allocation of nx labels among n samples on the finalized normalized matrix. P-values were exact permutation p-values from this enumeration, which removed Monte Carlo error and the 1/(B+1) resolution floor (Phipson and Smyth 2010). Ties were retained in the effect estimate and statistic.

Gene-level p-values were converted to Storey q-values with the plug-in estimate of π0 at a fixed λ=0.5 and a threshold of q<0.10 <!-- N-05 -->. We fixed λ at 0.5 before analysis, the value used throughout Storey (2002), rather than selecting it adaptively from the observed p-value distribution of strongly dependent tests of co-expressed genes. The plug-in estimator is derived under weak dependence among tests. We therefore refer to “genes meeting the Storey q rule” and reserve the contrast-level existence statement for the Higher Criticism label-permutation result. Complete per-gene results for every tested gene in each contrast are provided in Supplementary Data 1.

### Contrast-level omnibus inference

Higher Criticism was the primary omnibus statistic, with the RET-tumor contrast as the single primary contrast-level test and the other contrasts as secondary. The scan used empirical per-gene p-values referred to the pooled distribution of all genes' shuffle statistics, excluded p-values at or below 1/n (the HC+ form), and covered the smallest α0=0.1 fraction of the ordered p-values. It was selected to aggregate moderately small ordered p-values rather than make the contrast-level conclusion depend on one extreme gene or a fixed gene-count cutoff. Its p-value was calculated with plus-one counting against the contrast's own label-shuffle distribution, so the analytic independence and sparsity assumptions of the original Higher Criticism null were not required. Count statistics at fixed cutoffs and the maximum statistic were retained as descriptive rows, calibrated against the same shuffles with plus-one counting. The full rejection curve R(α) was retained to show how the displayed gene count varied across q thresholds.

Exhaustive recomputation of the genome-wide omnibus profiles over every label allocation was not computationally practical. For each contrast, 9,999 label shuffles were therefore generated and stored using seed 19860426 <!-- N-04, N-05 -->. Shuffling broke the association between group labels and expression while retaining each sample's joint expression profile and hence the dependence among genes. These shuffles provided the null distributions for the omnibus statistics and the plug-in π0 estimator and were reused unchanged for gene-set inference.


### Gene-set inference

We used the complete gene ranking rather than a thresholded list of differentially expressed genes. Genes were ranked by tie-averaged normal scores of the signed Brunner–Munzel statistic, and the in-house weighted running-sum statistic was evaluated with gseaParam=1 only at tie-block boundaries. Automated tests verified equality to the standard GSEA statistic for tie-free input (Subramanian et al. 2005). For each set, the normalized enrichment score (NES) was obtained by dividing the observed enrichment score by the mean absolute enrichment score among null values of the same sign; the same sign-specific divisor was applied to that set's null scores.

The gene-set null comprised these same 9,999 saved label shuffles for each contrast. Permuting sample labels rather than genes preserved the subject-level gene-dependence structure, and reusing the shuffle bank ensured that the omnibus and gene-set procedures were evaluated on the same label allocations. For an observed positive NES, the sign-conditional permutation p-value was (1 plus the number of null NES values at least as large as observed)/(1 plus the number of non-negative null NES values); the corresponding lower-tail counts were used for a negative NES. We applied the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure within each collection (Benjamini and Hochberg 1995), using q<0.10 without making a cross-collection claim. We did not estimate π0 at the set level; in this setting, the BH procedure is equivalent to setting π0=1. This choice avoided reliance on a potentially unstable plug-in estimate from the dependent set-level p-values.

Gene-set definitions were obtained from MSigDB (Subramanian et al. 2005) with msigdbr 26.1.0 (Dolgalev 2026), which provides MSigDB release 2026.1 (human) <!-- N-98 -->. Before the 15–500-gene size filter, the four collections were Hallmark (50 sets; Liberzon et al. 2015); C2 canonical pathways restricted to Reactome (Milacic et al. 2024), WikiPathways (Agrawal et al. 2024), KEGG MEDICUS (Kanehisa et al. 2025), BioCarta (Nishimura 2001), and PID (Schaefer et al. 2009) (3,910 sets; the legacy KEGG subcollection was excluded); C5 GO Biological Process (7,538 sets; Ashburner et al. 2000; The Gene Ontology Consortium 2026); and a radiation-curated subset of C2:CGP (28 sets) <!-- N-29 -->. The radiation curation rule was fixed before the reported set-level run <!-- N-55 -->. Source publications for the radiation-curated sets represented in the reported results are listed in Table S8.

As a single positive-control scenario, a one-set spike-in was constructed: the finalized normalized counts-per-million matrix for the BRAF-tumor contrast was taken as the starting point, and the values of the 195 HALLMARK_ADIPOGENESIS genes present in the matrix were multiplied by 1.15 in its nine High-AS samples <!-- N-30 -->. The complete ranking and gene-set procedure described above was then repeated with 9,999 label shuffles and diagnostic seed 19450809. The planted set's within-Hallmark rank was defined by ascending BH q-value.

For over-representation analysis, RET-tumor genes meeting Storey q<0.10 were split into higher-expression, lower-expression, and combined lists. One-sided hypergeometric tests used all genes tested in the RET-tumor contrast as the universe, with BH adjustment within each family-by-list combination. For a list of n genes, a set of K genes, and a universe of N genes, the expected overlap under this reference was nK/N. Within a family-by-list cell, the set with the smallest raw hypergeometric p-value was used when identifying a single leading descriptive example; adjusted q-values, not that descriptive ordering, carried the q<0.10 rule. In the terminology of Goeman and Bühlmann (2007), the statistic is competitive, but its gene-sampling p-value does not refer to subject-level label randomization.

### Complete-null assessment of the set-level procedure

Because genes are co-expressed and gene sets overlap, we assessed the complete-null operating characteristics of the set-level procedure on held-out data. For each contrast, 10,099 label shuffles were generated using seed 19450809, independently of the shuffles used for the reported inference <!-- N-06 -->. The first 9,999 shuffles formed a shared null pool, and the remaining 100 served as held-out pseudo-observations. For each pseudo-observation, the set-level enrichment scores were normalized against the shared pool, sign-conditional permutation p-values were calculated, the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure was applied within each collection, and discoveries were recorded at q<0.10.

For each contrast-by-collection cell, the proportion of pseudo-observations yielding at least one discovery estimated the probability of at least one false discovery under the complete null, where the false discovery rate and family-wise error rate coincide. We also reported the mean number of discoveries. As a descriptive summary only, we pooled the 100 any-discovery indicators from each of the 16 cells and reported their total and proportion. Clopper–Pearson intervals (Clopper and Pearson 1934) were calculated as descriptive reference intervals; because the 100 pseudo-observations shared one null pool, their discovery indicators were dependent, and these intervals do not have exact coverage for the shared-pool design.

Before the reported gene-set analysis, an earlier development assessment using a 999-shuffle reference pool yielded pooled complete-null rates of 0.140 and 0.221 for two pooled tail-ratio procedures, with a maximum cell-specific rate of 0.44 <!-- N-58 -->. The per-set permutation procedure with within-collection Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment yielded 0.045 and was selected <!-- N-57 -->. The final assessment described above used a 9,999-shuffle reference pool and yielded a descriptive pooled rate of 0.064 <!-- N-24 -->; one cell had a rate of 0.18 <!-- N-25 -->. The assessment characterized behavior under the complete null and did not cover partial alternatives. Cell-level findings are reported in Supplementary Results.

### Concordance between the RET and BRAF contrasts within each tissue

For each tissue, the RET and BRAF profiles of signed gene-level Brunner–Munzel statistics were intersected and compared using Spearman correlation. For the null reference, 9,999 label shuffles were generated separately from those used for omnibus and gene-set inference, using a distinct seed for each contrast derived from the base seed 19450809 <!-- N-73 -->. At each iteration, labels were shuffled independently in the RET and BRAF contrasts before their signed-statistic profiles were correlated. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles formed the empirical reference interval, and a two-sided Monte Carlo p-value was calculated with plus-one counting. The normal-tissue comparison addressed a specified hypothesis, whereas the tumor-tissue comparison was descriptive. This null represents the case in which neither stratum carries structure aligned with the band labels. A correlation outside the reference interval does not distinguish exposure-associated structure from a covariate shared across strata.

### REO panel construction and intermediate-band application

Under the within-sample gene-ordering framework (Geman et al. 2004; Wang et al. 2015; Guan et al. 2016), TPM was recalculated from the selected count assay using exon-union gene lengths because comparisons across different genes require length normalization. Among genes with an exon-union length annotation, we ranked genes by the absolute distance of the Brunner–Munzel effect from 0.5 and took the top 500 without using the q<0.10 list. We divided these genes into higher-expression (effect >0.5) and lower-expression (effect <0.5) classes and retained a gene for pairing only when its log2 TPM was finite in every dose-zero and High-AS construction sample. Every eligible higher-by-lower gene pair was evaluated.

For a gene pair, r was the within-sample log2-TPM difference between its higher- and lower-expression genes. The dead zone was |r|<log2(1.2). We defined the dose-zero reference sign by the majority sign outside the dead zone and allowed at most one opposite-sign dose-zero sample. The 10th percentile of |r| among dose-zero samples, taken as the ceiling(0.1 × n0)-th smallest value (the second smallest among the 12 dose-zero samples), also had to be at least log2(1.5). In High-AS samples, the reversal rate is the number outside the dead zone with a sign opposite to the dose-zero reference divided by the total number of High-AS construction samples. A pair qualified when this rate was greater than 50% but less than 100% <!-- N-74 -->. Qualified pairs were ranked by the absolute difference between the dose-zero and High-AS median r values. Greedy selection prohibited gene reuse and excluded a pair whose r profile had an absolute Spearman correlation of at least 0.75 with a retained pair <!-- N-75 -->. The target was 10 pairs <!-- N-37 -->.

The reversal score counted pairs outside the dead zone that opposed the dose-zero reference sign. The classification cutoff was set at the maximum reversal score among dose-zero construction samples. A sample was classified as positive only when its score exceeded that cutoff. We did not change the panel or its classification rule after construction.

Binary and graded readouts were defined for the panel. In the dose-zero and High-AS construction groups, the binary readout characterized separation between the groups used to construct the panel. Demonstrating this separation was a prerequisite for application elsewhere, but it was not an unbiased estimate of classification performance because the same cases informed pair selection and the dose-zero cases set the cutoff. We then applied the classification rule unchanged to Low-AS and Mid-AS RET fusion-positive PTCs and treated the resulting classifications as descriptive application results.

We summarized graded scores across all four bands, with dose-zero and High-AS serving as construction anchors and the panel applied without refitting to Low-AS and Mid-AS. Within the two intermediate bands, the one-sided Brunner–Munzel comparison assessed whether scores tended to be higher in Mid-AS than in Low-AS. Throughout the analysis, Brunner–Munzel permutation distributions were enumerated exactly when the number of label allocations C(n,nx) did not exceed 100,000,000 and were otherwise approximated by Monte Carlo sampling. Exhaustive enumeration here would have required 8,597,496,600 label allocations, so 999,999 Monte Carlo allocations with seed 19860426 and plus-one counting were used instead <!-- N-91 -->. This comparison did not test linearity, four-band monotonicity, or a dose–response form. Neither it nor the four-band display constituted independent validation because the panel and score direction came from the construction groups.

### Ancillary REO quality and purity diagnostics

As a descriptive quality check, the same PC-OD input construction was applied separately within the Low-AS and Mid-AS RET fusion-positive PTCs. Flags from this screen were reported but were not used to exclude cases; every intermediate-band case remained in the REO application.

For cases with matched normal tissue, relative purity was estimated on a common scale by pooling paired cases across all four RET groups in one contamDE-lm proportion run separate from the main-cohort estimation, and the resulting scores were summarized in each band by their median. Cases without matched normal tissue remained in the REO application but did not enter the purity analyses.

Associations between relative purity and REO score were summarized using Spearman correlations separately within Low-AS and Mid-AS cases and after pooling the two bands. In the pooled intermediate-band cases, the band–score and band–purity Spearman correlations were also calculated. To describe the band–score association after rank-scale adjustment for purity, the partial Spearman coefficient was calculated by correlating residuals from separate linear projections of band rank and score rank on purity rank.

In a separate diagnostic permutation procedure, the Low-AS and Mid-AS labels were permuted 9,999 times using seed 19860426 while retaining each sample's observed score–purity pair. With Mid-AS coded above Low-AS, an upper-tail p-value for a positive partial coefficient was calculated using plus-one counting. Because this scheme also breaks the band–purity association, the resulting p-value describes the band–score association without holding purity fixed.

As a further descriptive comparison, the pooled Low-AS and Mid-AS cases were split into two strata at the median of the common-scale purity and, within each stratum, a one-sided Brunner–Munzel test assessed whether REO scores tended to be higher in Mid-AS than in Low-AS cases. At these group sizes, the permutation distribution was enumerated exactly. None of these purity analyses was designated as a confirmatory test of an AS-band association independent of purity.

### External gene-list comparison

Two evidence classes were cross-referenced. The qRT-PCR-validated class comprised the separate tumor and normal lists from Abend et al. (2013), the tumor-minus-normal paired-difference list from Abend et al. (2012), the normal-tissue list from Dom et al. (2012), and CLIP2 as a single-gene tumor reference. The Abend et al. (2012) list had no matching estimand in the present contrasts and was designated indirect. The multivariate-signature class comprised the 50-gene shared-tissue, 45-gene normal-tissue, and 64-gene tumor-tissue transcriptomic signatures reported in Supplementary Tables S2, S4, and S6 of Ory et al. (2026) <!-- N-85 -->. It also comprised the 106-gene signature (109 probes) reported in Table S3 of Ugolin et al. (2011), which discriminated post-Chornobyl from sporadic PTCs in a reanalysis of the series of Detours et al. (2007). The direction of each gene was taken from the sign of the reported log-expression difference, and three genes represented by two probes were entered once <!-- N-101 -->. It further comprised the 322-probe signature reported in Supplementary Table 3 of Ory et al. (2011), which discriminated radiation-induced from sporadic follicular adenomas and papillary carcinomas arising after external radiotherapy in childhood. The direction of each probe was taken from the sign of the reported log-expression difference, nine probes that hybridized two paralogous genes were entered as one row per gene, and probes without a gene symbol were kept under their accession and did not contribute to n <!-- N-99 -->. We kept these signatures separate from the qRT-PCR-validated lists because they were selected by multivariate procedures, age- and sex-adjusted sPLS-DA or DIABLO (Ory et al. 2026) and a combinatorial PCA-based classification (Ory et al. 2011; Ugolin et al. 2011), rather than by gene-wise testing followed by qRT-PCR confirmation.

Published symbols were mapped to the GENCODE v36 annotation and, for each source list and contrast, k of n genes were counted, where n is the number of listed genes present among the contrast's tested genes and k is the number also present in its q<0.10 list. Entries absent from GENCODE v36 or not retained in a contrast-specific tested set did not contribute to n. The shared-tissue list of Ory et al. (2026) was compared with both tissues. The normal- and tumor-tissue lists were additionally labeled as tissue-matched or cross-tissue for each contrast. For each overlap, the current contrast's effect direction was recorded and, when the source report supplied a direction, compared with it; cross-tissue direction comparisons remained explicitly labeled as such. Because the source studies differed in tissue, platform, contrast, driver control, and list-construction procedure, no enrichment statistic was calculated. We reported every overlap count descriptively and did not use the counts or direction comparisons to change the inferential claims.

### Software, seeds, and reproducibility

The container was built from the immutable Ubuntu 24.04 base image (noble-20260410) and matching apt snapshot, R 4.5.3 (R Core Team 2026) was compiled against reference BLAS/LAPACK 3.12.0, and R packages were installed from the 2026-04-09 CRAN and Bioconductor 3.22 snapshots <!-- N-79 -->. Bit-level reproducibility was targeted for linux/amd64.

In-house components included exact-enumeration and Monte Carlo Brunner–Munzel statistics, the Storey plug-in estimator, tie-block enrichment, DEGES-MUREN normalization, the contamDE-lm proportion score, the principal-component outlier procedure, and REO panel construction. External packages included edgeR, SummarizedExperiment, msigdbr, limma, GenomicDataCommons, rtracklayer, Rcpp (Eddelbuettel and François 2011), and MASS. Packages are cited at first use, and their full version numbers are listed in Table S2.

## Supplementary Results

### Gene-set analysis and complete-null assessment

After applying the 15–500-gene size filter, 6,141–6,242 sets were tested per contrast across the four collections <!-- N-28 -->. No set met the within-collection Benjamini–Hochberg threshold q<0.10 in any of the 16 contrast-by-collection cells. The smallest adjusted value was q=0.114 in the BRAF-tumor radiation-curated cell (Table S3; Supplementary Data 2) <!-- N-27, N-28 -->.

In the held-out complete-null assessment, the per-cell proportion of pseudo-observations that produced at least one discovery ranged from 0.01 to 0.18, and 13 of the 16 cells were at or below the nominal 0.10 level <!-- N-24 -->. The BRAF-tumor radiation-curated cell had a rate of 0.10 (descriptive 95% Clopper–Pearson interval, 0.049–0.176), and the BRAF-normal radiation-curated cell had a rate of 0.12 (0.064–0.200) <!-- N-26 -->. Only the BRAF-normal/Hallmark cell had a descriptive interval lying entirely above 0.10 (rate 0.18; 0.110–0.269) <!-- N-25 -->. Pooled across the 16 cells, 102 of 1,600 pseudo-observations produced at least one discovery (descriptive pooled rate, 0.064; Fig. S2; Table S4) <!-- N-24 -->.

In the single positive-control scenario, the expression values for 195 genes from HALLMARK_ADIPOGENESIS were multiplied by 1.15 in the nine High-AS BRAF-tumor samples. The planted set had a normalized enrichment score of 2.28, permutation p=0.0002, and q=0.0101, ranking first among 50 Hallmark sets <!-- N-30, N-31 -->. No other Hallmark set met q<0.10 <!-- N-32 -->.

### Over-representation annotation of the RET-tumor list

No set in any family met the family-by-list q<0.10 rule for genes expressed at higher levels in High-AS cases. For genes expressed at lower levels, 12 Hallmark, 105 C2 canonical-pathway, 205 C5 GO Biological Process, and 7 radiation-curated sets met the rule. For the combined list, the counts were 6, 37, 71, and 2 in the same order (Table S5) <!-- N-59 -->.

Among the sets meeting the rule for the lower-expression list, E2F targets had 46 of its 199 genes in the list, G2M checkpoint 41 of 198 <!-- N-61 -->, and Reactome DNA repair 68 of 322 <!-- N-62 -->. The radiation-curated set with the smallest raw hypergeometric p-value had 46 of its 126 genes in the lower-expression list, compared with 6.4 expected under the gene-sampling reference <!-- N-60 -->.

### Sex-chromosome annotation of the RET-tumor list

Among the 1,765 RET-tumor genes meeting the Storey q<0.10 rule (Supplementary Data 1), 57 were X-linked: 36 were expressed at higher and 21 at lower levels in High-AS cases; no Y-linked gene met the rule <!-- N-82 -->. The 15,621 genes tested in the RET-tumor contrast included 572 X-linked and two Y-linked genes <!-- N-82 -->.

### REO pair construction

The top-500 construction ranking contained 318 genes expressed at higher and 182 at lower levels in High-AS RET fusion-positive PTCs <!-- N-35 -->. One higher-expression gene had zero TPM in at least one construction sample and therefore failed the finite-log2-TPM requirement, leaving 317 higher- and 182 lower-expression genes eligible for pairing. Of the 57,694 pairs that combined one higher-expression gene with one lower-expression gene, 153 met all three selection rules <!-- N-35 -->. Across these 153 pairs, the absolute shift in median within-sample log2-TPM difference ranged from 1.159 to 4.700, and the High-AS reversal rate ranged from 0.53 to 0.87 <!-- N-36 -->. Greedy selection with the gene-reuse and correlation restrictions produced the final 10-pair panel. The maximum reversal score among dose-zero construction samples was 2; consequently, scores greater than 2 were classified as positive (Table S7) <!-- N-38 -->.


### Ancillary REO quality and purity diagnostics

The ancillary PC-OD screen identified no outliers among the intermediate-band tumors (Low-AS, 0/17; Mid-AS, 0/19) <!-- N-43 -->.

Relative purity could be estimated for 15 Low-AS and 16 Mid-AS tumors <!-- N-44 -->. The other two Low-AS and three Mid-AS tumors had no matched normal sample and therefore no purity score. In the pooled paired RET purity run, median relative purity scores were 0.690, 0.704, 0.739, and 0.814 in the dose-zero, Low-AS, Mid-AS, and High-AS bands, respectively <!-- N-44 -->.

Within the intermediate bands, Spearman correlations between purity and REO score were 0.684 in Low-AS cases, 0.345 in Mid-AS cases, and 0.538 when pooled <!-- N-45 -->. The unadjusted band–score correlation was 0.142, the band–purity correlation was 0.036 <!-- N-48 -->, and the partial Spearman coefficient after adjustment for purity rank was 0.146 (descriptive one-sided permutation p=0.2162) <!-- N-46 -->. Purity-stratified one-sided comparisons of Mid-AS with Low-AS (two strata split at the median purity) yielded p=0.3069 (θ=0.580) in the lower-purity stratum and p=0.4197 (θ=0.532) in the higher-purity stratum <!-- N-47 -->.
